Air Ping Pong

Your open hand is the racket. Your palm is the red forehand rubber, the back of your hand is the black backhand rubber. The ball comes off whichever side it touches, at the angle you hold it, with the speed and spin of your swing. It is real collision physics, with no preset shots.

  • Face angle. Tilt your fingertips back toward you (palm turning up) to open the face and lift the ball. Tilt them toward the screen to close it and keep the ball low. Turn the palm left or right to aim.
  • Swing. Push toward the screen for power. Brush up through the ball for topspin (it dips onto the table), brush down for backspin (it floats), brush sideways for sidespin (it curves). Hold still to block.
  • Incoming spin. Topspin jumps off your racket high and long, so close the face. Backspin drops into the net, so open the face and lift. The label above the ball says which one is coming.

The little ring on the table shows where the ball would land if it met your racket as you hold it right now: green lands, yellow lands only with the assist, red misses. First to 11, two ahead. To serve, swing through the ball floating in front of you.

When the camera starts, show it your open palm (like a high five) so it learns which side is which.
